Mention the types of Returns to scale.2 marksAsked 3× · 1 board exam

Asked in: E26-2 (Q19), MQP24-1 (Q18), MQP-5 (Q19)
Also asked as: "State the types of Returns to scale." (MQP-5)

Board answer key · E26-2
  1. Increasing Returns to Scale [IRS]
  2. Constant Returns to Scale [CRS]
  3. Decreasing Returns to Scale [DRS]
Meaning of each · NCERT
  • CRS: a proportional increase in all inputs increases output by the same proportion.
  • IRS: a proportional increase in all inputs increases output by a larger proportion.
  • DRS: a proportional increase in all inputs increases output by a smaller proportion.

Example: if all inputs are doubled and output exactly doubles, it is CRS. If output more than doubles, it is IRS. If output less than doubles, it is DRS.

What are long run costs?2 marksAsked 2× · 1 board exam

Asked in: E26-1 (Q19), MQP-3 (Q19)
Also covers: "What is Long run Average Cost? Write its formula." (2 marks, MQP-4), the "LRAC" match item (MQP24-1) and "Explain the long run costs." (4 marks, MQP24-3)

Board answer key · E26-1

(a) Long-run Average Cost [LAC or LRAC]
(b) Long-run Marginal Cost [LMC or LRMC]

Meaning and formulas · NCERT
  • In the long run all inputs are variable, so there are no fixed costs. Total cost and total variable cost are the same.
  • Long run average cost (LRAC) is the cost per unit of output: LRAC = TC ÷ q
  • Long run marginal cost (LRMC) is the change in total cost per unit change in output: LRMC = TC at q1 units − TC at (q1 − 1) units
  • Both curves are U-shaped. LRMC cuts LRAC from below at the minimum point of LRAC. The falling part of LRAC shows IRS, the minimum point shows CRS and the rising part shows DRS.
Give the meaning of Short-run and Long-run.2 marksAsked 2× · 1 board exam

Asked in: E25-2 (Q20), MQP-2 (Q19)
Also asked as: "Give the meaning of short-run and long-run concepts." (MQP-2)

Board answer key · E25-2

Short run: In the short run, at least one of the factors (labour or capital) cannot be varied, and therefore remains fixed. In order to vary the output level, the firm can vary only the other factor.

Long run: In the long run, all factors of production can be varied. A firm, in order to produce different levels of output in the long run, may vary both the inputs simultaneously. So, in the long run, there is no fixed factor.

NCERT adds: short run and long run are not defined in days, months or years. A period is long run or short run depending only on whether all inputs can be varied.

_________ is the set of all possible combinations of two inputs that yield the same maximum possible level of output.1 mark · Fill in the blankAsked 3× · 2 board exams

Asked in: E26-1 (Q7), E25-3 (Q7), MQP-3 (Q6)
Also covers: "What is Isoquant?" (2 marks, MQP24-2)

Board answer key · E26-1, E25-3

Isoquant

2-mark version · NCERT

An isoquant is the set of all possible combinations of the two inputs (labour and capital) that yield the same maximum possible level of output. Each isoquant represents a particular level of output and is labelled with that amount. Isoquants slope downward: to produce the same output with more of one input, less of the other is needed.

NCERT example (Table 3.1): 10 units of output can be produced with (4L, 1K), (2L, 2K) or (1L, 4K). All three lie on the isoquant q = 10.

Micro Ch 4 · Theory of the Firm under Perfect Competition2-mark slots: usually Q20–Q21

State the conditions needed for profit maximization by a firm under perfect competition.2 marksAsked 3× · 2 board exams

Asked in: E26-1 (Q20), E26-2 (Q20), MQP-2 (Q20)
Also covers: the one-off longer versions: "Write about the third condition of profit maximization of the firm under perfect competition with the help of diagrams." (4 marks, MQP-5) and "Write a short note on profit maximization of a firm under the following conditions a) P=MC b) MC must be none decreasing at q0" (6 marks, E25-3)

Board answer key · E26-1
  1. The price P must be equal to MC (P = MC).
  2. Marginal cost must be non-decreasing at q0.
  3. In the short run, price must be greater than or equal to the average variable cost (P ≥ AVC), and in the long run, price must be greater than or equal to the average cost (P ≥ AC).

Why P = MC (NCERT): profit is highest where MR = MC. For a perfectly competitive firm MR = P, so the condition becomes P = MC.

What is monotonic preference?2 marksAsked 3× · 1 board exam

Asked in: E25-3 (Q18), MQP24-1 (Q17), MQP-2 (Q18)

Board answer key · E25-3

Monotonic preferences imply that between any two indifference curves, the bundles on the one which lies above are preferred to the bundles on the one which lies below.

Here the consumer will not remain indifferent between two combinations of commodities when he has an opportunity to have more quantity in one combination than the other.

Definition · NCERT

A consumer's preferences are monotonic if, between any two bundles, she prefers the bundle that has more of at least one of the goods and no less of the other good. Example: (10, 10) is preferred to (10, 9).
